The Easiest Time To Add Guards Is While We're Already There

Guards are rarely the reason someone calls us first — they're something we bring up on-site once we're already looking at your eavestrough for another reason. If you're booking any of these, it's worth asking about guards in the same visit:

  • Getting new eavestrough installed — the most cost-effective time to add guards, since we're already forming and mounting the run
  • Booking an eavestrough repair — if debris buildup contributed to the issue, guards can prevent it recurring
  • Scheduling an eavestrough cleaning — if you're cleaning more than once or twice a year, guards cut that frequency dramatically

Do You Actually Need Them?

Not every home does. Guards make the most sense if you've got mature trees overhanging the roof, you're dealing with frequent clogs or overflow, or you'd simply rather not be up a ladder every season. If none of that applies to your property, we'll tell you honestly it's not a priority — we're not going to add it to every quote by default.

What Guards Actually Do

A gutter guard sits over the open top of your eavestrough, letting water in while blocking leaves, needles, and larger debris. They don't eliminate maintenance entirely — fine grit and seed pods can still build up over years — but they cut the frequency of clean-outs dramatically and reduce the risk of a clogged system overflowing during a heavy rain.

On guard pricing: cost depends on your total linear footage and whether guards are going on new eavestrough or retrofitted onto an existing system. Bundled with an install, repair, or cleaning visit, it's typically a modest add to that quote rather than a separate call-out. You'll get an exact price after a quick on-site look.
